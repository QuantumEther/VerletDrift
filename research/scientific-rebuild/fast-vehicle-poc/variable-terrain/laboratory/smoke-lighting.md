# Smoke layering, lighting and terrain effects

Smoke now renders at transparent order 3 after road marks (order 2), with depth testing retained and depth writes disabled. Opaque terrain, bodywork and wheels still occlude it. This repairs the explicit road-overlay ordering conflict; it is not order-independent transparency for every transparent vehicle material.

The particle shader receives sun direction, intensity and color, ambient light color/intensity and the environment intensity setting. A rounded-particle lighting approximation provides soft density shading; the renderer's tone-mapping/exposure and output color-space chunks are applied. Sun direction is transformed into the active camera frame before drawing. HDR contribution is an ambient-intensity approximation, not directional PMREM sampling or volumetric light transport.

Four pooled broad shadow patches aggregate live cloud positions/opacity per wheel. They project away from the sun, sample analytic terrain height and gradient, and blend softly over ground/marks while depth testing against opaque geometry. At most eight terrain samples and four additional small draws occur per graphical frame, independent of particle count; no physics queries or solver changes. This approximation follows the local tangent plane and can intersect sharply curved terrain. It is not a full smoke shadow map; transparent vehicle parts remain a sorting limitation.

All six surface types retain supported-contact, sliding-speed and irreversible-loss gates. Asphalt/concrete use gray-white smoke, gravel/dirt brown dust, grass muted soil/vegetation tint and rock gray fine dust. There is no individually simulated vegetation debris. Ordinary rolling and airborne spinning remain gated out. Existing controls, particle budgets and world coordinates are preserved.

Reproduce unit checks with `node --test research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/laboratory/*.test.mjs`. For controlled real WebGL pixel/screenshot checks run `node research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/laboratory/lit-smoke-browser.mjs asphalt` (or concrete, gravel, dirt, grass, rock). Inputs are explicitly controlled contact-loss fixtures, not live gameplay. Each capture includes a transparent road-mark proxy and terrain plane, real production particles and projected shadows. Paired on/off captures and pixel counts are `results/lit-smoke-<surface>*`. Browser backend is Chromium/SwiftShader; hardware GPU cost and varied HDR images have not been measured. This validation establishes rendered output for the tested lighting configuration, not every lighting/terrain condition.
