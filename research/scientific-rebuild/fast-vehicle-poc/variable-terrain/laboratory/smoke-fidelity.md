# Smoke illumination and distributed shadows

The previous shader collapsed sun, ambient and environment intensity into one color. Its shadow used one averaged circle per wheel, losing the trail's spatial distribution. The new implementation shares the graphics controller's already-decoded HDR source texture (no duplicate load/decode), samples it in world directions for broad ambient illumination, and receives actual sun color/intensity/direction and hemisphere light. Renderer tone mapping/exposure remains shared. A forward-biased scattering approximation and optical-depth attenuation give illuminated and internally shaded regions. This is an efficient two-dimensional density approximation, not radiative-transfer simulation; scattering coefficients are visual choices, not measured tire properties.

Each wheel's cloud is divided into eight age sections. Actual live world positions, sizes, heights and opacity determine their projected centers, widths and optical density. Four pooled meshes blend eight soft lobes each; sun elevation shifts shadows, height broadens them, and weak sun reduces their weight. No per-particle mesh or shadow map is introduced. At most 36 analytic terrain samples per graphical update and four shadow draws, with the existing 600-particle maximum. Tangent-plane projection still approximates curved terrain. Near-horizon projection is bounded and below-horizon shadows are disabled. No physical state, solver or drivetrain changes.

`smoke-fidelity-browser.mjs asphalt sun`, `... asphalt overcast`, and `... asphalt low` use the actual production effect, loaded German Town EXR and standard-lit reference geometry. On/off screenshots and pixel records are `results/fidelity-smoke-*`; these are controlled constitutive inputs, not vehicle gameplay. They passed actual WebGL pixel-difference checks on Chromium/SwiftShader. The reference object is explicitly a material/lighting proxy, not a vehicle. A separate `intense-tire-smoke-stationary-browser.mjs` run captures real Schweinemotorwerke physics. The 33 Node tests pass. Hardware performance, directional HDR comparisons of the full vehicle in all three conditions, and volumetric self-shadow accuracy remain unverified.
