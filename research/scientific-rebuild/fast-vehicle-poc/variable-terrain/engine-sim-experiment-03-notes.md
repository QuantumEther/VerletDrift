# Natural procedural audio — Experiment 03

Serve the repository root with `python3 -m http.server 8000` and open `research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/engine-sim-hybrid-v3.html` through that server. Use Audio to select Procedural, Sample or Hybrid; Synthesis switches original/refined procedural voicing. The expandable component controls adjust exhaust, intake, mechanical tick and pops. Gains and voicing transitions are smoothed in the audio worklet.

The existing pipeline applies asymmetric exhaust saturation, adds high-frequency rasp and crackle, mixes pops and mechanical tick, and soft-clips the output. These are potential sources of coloration; their individual perceptual contribution has not been independently established. The refined setting reduces the exhaust shaper drive, mid-frequency emphasis, rasp, crackle and pop amplitude while retaining the firing-event and architecture-specific timing. It does not change the sample playback algorithm. There is no new random modulation or vehicle physics.

The layer controls adjust the final procedural mix; turbo, starter and driveline sounds remain separately audible. The original setting restores the prior worklet's voicing when all layer gains are at 100%. Both settings pass through Experiment 01's existing mix, reverb, compressor and safety output stage.

The changes are hypotheses for listening, not proof of greater realism. The single recording's unknown engine and limited RPM coverage remain; no multi-RPM recording, anti-aliasing redesign, phase locking, or independent component isolation study has been performed. In particular, the sample-only path retains the previous experiment's processing and should be judged against that reference. A level-matched listening comparison and spectral/aliasing measurements remain necessary before choosing defaults.

A Chromium browser smoke run executed 23 cases covering the three audio modes, acceleration/deceleration, stop/restart, five engine profiles and blend extremes. The measured output was finite and unclipped; the largest observed sample peak was 0.330. This smoke test does not independently measure distortion, aliasing, component balance or perceptual naturalness, and it does not establish that the new sound is an improvement. Reproduce it with `node research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/engine-sim-experiment-03-test.mjs`; its result file is written to `/tmp/experiment03-results.json`.

## Follow-up: reduce radio-like noise and extend sample use

The refined voice now uses a linear, lower-drive exhaust body before the existing final soft safety stages, reduces rasp by 90% and crackle by 98%, and reduces mechanical tick. Pops default to zero and remain adjustable. The original voicing remains selectable; set all four component sliders to 100% to compare its original mix. These are targeted reductions in potential noise and nonlinear coloration, not verified recordings of real exhaust acoustics.

The single recording provides a coherent mono idle-like texture, and its existing 3.9-second crossfaded loop can also supply mid-range texture via playback-rate adjustment. The new **Sample range** selector offers Conservative (existing fade, 1.7–2.7×) and Extended (3.0–4.5×) hybrid crossfades. Extended is an optional listening experiment: because the source RPM, engine identity and load are unknown, additional transposition shifts every recorded resonance and may sound less realistic. Sample mode remains the unmodified recording-only comparison. The recording cannot independently supply authentic high-RPM load, intake, gear or transient recordings. Recording several RPM/load points with known source information remains the strongest next step.

No listening judgment is asserted by these changes. In particular, neither reduced synthetic noise nor increased sample contribution guarantees a more realistic sound for every profile or RPM.

After this follow-up, the 23-case Chromium audio smoke test passed again; its maximum measured post-output peak was 0.228, with no nonfinite or clipped samples in the measured windows. This is a signal-safety check, not a listening assessment.

## Redline recording and a second use of the sample

The previous Extended mode made the recording disappear above 4.5 times the unverified reference RPM, approximately 3,488 rpm at the default 775 rpm reference. The V8 reaches about 6,905 rpm in the test, so that crossfade fully removed it near redline. **Full RPM** now keeps the original sample layer active through redline and raises the default recorded blend to 85%. Conservative and Extended remain available for direct comparison. This deliberately exposes the limitations of roughly 8.9× transposition near V8 redline rather than claiming the clip contains a real high-RPM recording.

A separate **Texture** control adds up to 25% of a 500 Hz high-pass version of the same playback to Hybrid. It is a second spectral use of the recording, not an independently recorded load layer. It shares the same source and playback phase, adds no independent oscillation, and defaults to zero so it cannot silently add hiss. Filtering changes phase and may color the sound; use the slider to decide whether it helps. Sample-only mode continues to use the original unfiltered sample path and does not include the texture layer.

In a focused Chromium test at 6,905 rpm, Full RPM reported sample gain 0.802 and texture gain 0.201 with Texture at 100%; switching to Conservative reduced sample gain to 0.0001. Sample-only mode disabled the texture path. Output was finite and the measured instantaneous peak was below 1. These figures confirm routing, not that the recording is perceptually louder or more realistic; human listening and matched-level comparison are still needed.

The 23-case audio smoke test also passed with Full RPM as the default; maximum measured post-output peak was 0.241. The focused redline/Texture test supplements that smoke run. No human listening evaluation was available.

## REC25 and recorded rev experiments

The laboratory's **Recording** selector now switches between the existing REC194 loop and the newly provided REC25 loop without resetting the engine. REC25 is mono 44.1 kHz / 16-bit and 5.0155 seconds; the steady section used here starts at 0.760 s and ends at 4.102 s, with 0.120 s overlap (3.222 s final loop). The selected overlap had approximately 0.940 normalized correlation. Both loops are independently normalized to the existing fixed RMS target and crossfaded when switching.

In 0.5–4.5 s Welch analysis, REC25 had approximately 78.6% of measured spectral power below 100 Hz and 3.2% above 1 kHz; REC194 had approximately 61.2% below 100 Hz and 12.9% above 1 kHz. This supports the observation that REC25 has a bassier spectral balance, although microphone placement and recording levels may affect the comparison. Both sound files have mostly steady tonal components around 50–55 and 75–85 Hz. Their engine identity, cylinder count, actual RPM, and load are unknown. They should not be treated as two verified RPM points or an exact performance curve.

The provided `ryton-fissore-magnum_revving.mp3` decodes to about 14.31 seconds and has varied amplitude and spectral content rather than a calibrated monotonic RPM sweep. **Rev section** offers three candidate 2.8-second excerpts (1.05–3.85, 3.7–6.5, and 7.0–9.8 seconds) for listening. **Rev clip** sets their blend (default 25%). When throttle is pressed while running in Hybrid mode, one selected excerpt plays at its original speed; its start/end are faded, and releasing throttle fades it out. There is no repeated trigger for keyboard auto-repeat. The recording is not continuously synchronized to the simulated RPM and may sound like a different engine, duplicate the loop or conflict with the procedural engine. Choose a section by ear or set Rev clip to 0% if it detracts. There are no claims of authentic acceleration timing, and sample-only mode deliberately leaves the rev overlay off for comparison. A missing MP3 leaves the steady loop and procedural sound usable.

To verify the two recordings, selection, rev trigger/release, the three candidate offsets and sample-only isolation, run `node research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/engine-sim-recordings-test.mjs`. This uses actual Chromium Web Audio; no automated result substitutes for listening. The most useful future recording remains a set of RPM- and load-labeled clips of the same engine, including separately recorded start/stop and throttle transients.
