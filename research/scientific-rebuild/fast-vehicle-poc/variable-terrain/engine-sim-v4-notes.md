# Recorded blend experiment 04

Open `engine-sim-hybrid-v4.html` alongside the existing media over HTTP/HTTPS. Previous experiments are preserved. The selected REC194/REC25 loop remains the primary RPM-following source. A new **Recorded bed** slider blends a separately prepared 6.425–11.025 s sustained MP3 section using a 180 ms raised-cosine overlap and fixed RMS normalization. This section was selected from the acoustic segmentation for its relatively strong periodicity, not verified engine RPM or load.

The bed uses a slowly changing, bounded square-root RPM ratio (0.6–2.5×), unlike the primary loop's direct ratio. This preserves more of its original spectral texture at high engine RPM, but deliberately sacrifices RPM-faithful firing frequency: it is a background texture, not a physically synchronized second engine. Different recordings can interfere or sound like two engines. The slider defaults to 35% and can be zeroed for comparison.

Its gain responds smoothly to the existing load telemetry. Low-pass cutoff follows load (800–3000 Hz, low resonance), giving quieter low-load texture and more upper content under load. This is audio styling, not measured acoustic calibration. Bed entry modestly reduces the primary layer's gain; during a rev transient the bed is ducked to 60% of its current contribution. Gain smoothing is 100 ms, rate/filter smoothing 120 ms. The three existing MP3 transient choices retain their native pitch and fade envelopes. Sources are native Web Audio buffer players, with no granular scheduler or additional synthetic noise.

The bed is silent in Procedural and Sample modes. Recording selection and existing conservative/extended/full RPM controls remain available. The original sample-only comparison is retained. Existing output protection remains in place; overlapping recordings are not guaranteed to be loudness-matched or physically phase-coherent.

Validation command: `node research/scientific-rebuild/fast-vehicle-poc/variable-terrain/engine-sim-blend-test.mjs`. Chromium checks recording changes, all three transient offsets, release, bed presence, zero-slider mute and Sample-mode bed isolation. These are functionality/finite-level checks, not a listening verdict or a comprehensive latency/aliasing study. Human listening is needed to decide whether the additional layer is richer or simply masks the primary recording. Exact RPM/load-labeled recordings of the same engine remain preferable to inferred layers from unknown sources.
