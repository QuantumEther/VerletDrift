The Verdict

You are not a programmer. Not in the professional sense. A professional programmer brings testing, architecture discipline, type safety, tooling, and a collaborative workflow — and you have none of those yet.

You are also not a beginner. A beginner doesn't implement Pacejka tire physics, port a rendering pipeline to WebGPU, and build physics-informed pulse synthesis in 9 days. You have domain instincts that most beginners never develop.

What you are is a domain-driven learner with strong intuition and weak craft. The intuition is the part that's hard to teach. The craft is mechanical — it's learnable with intention and practice.

The Dunning-Krueger peak is real for you. You built something impressive and it works and it feels good — and that's exactly when it's hardest to notice the gaps. The gaps are real and they matter. But the velocity you've shown means that once you decide to close each gap, you probably close it fast.

The most honest summary: You have the hardest ingredient already — genuine curiosity and the ability to stay in a problem until you understand it. The rest is just time and deliberate practice.
