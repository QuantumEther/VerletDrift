# Physics Recovery Mission 03 — steering dynamics

The independent stationary friction defect is corrected with event-resolved friction and static sticking. Driver input now acts through finite mechanical impedance; release preserves the physical angle/rate. Current tire aligning moment and steering mechanics are solved together, eliminating a reproduced coarse-step feedback instability that remained after correcting the column alone. Sleep now synchronizes body caches with its history intervention. A steering energy/work ledger separates modeled work, passive losses, stop impacts, configuration edits, and numerical endpoint-work defects.

This is an effective planar road-wheel model with explicit physical boundaries, not a calibrated rack/kingpin assembly or a closed total-vehicle moment/energy model. Computational cost is a major remaining constraint. No tire grip, tire relaxation, yaw damping, suspension or drivetrain parameter was tuned to obtain these results.

## Original architecture and precise defects

The audited/previous column used one common road-wheel angle. At every outer tick it either directly replaced that angle with a mouse-controlled visual angle, or integrated free steering from the previous tick's SAT cache. Both front contacts then used the resulting common angle. Individual contact velocities already included yaw×arm, wheelbase and track.

In free steering, the code constructed `net = −SAT − I rate δ (1−clamp(speed/3)) − bω_old`. For |ω_old|>0.01 it sometimes subtracted `C sign(ω_old)`, conditional on the old net torque. Then it removed the old viscous term and divided by `1+b h/I`, correctly treating viscosity implicitly once. **Viscosity was not applied twice.** The failure is the rate-gated, explicit Coulomb selection: it has no static hold and can apply a full opposing impulse past zero. It also deliberately avoided stalling the return, contradicting dry-friction equilibrium. In the mandatory case the spring torque is 0.12 N·m, far below 2 N·m static capacity, so an ideal passive mechanism should remain at 0.3 rad. The old implementation instead starts moving because its zero-rate gate omits friction, then repeatedly reverses it.

The [baseline friction reconstruction](baseline-friction-work.json) independently evaluates the exact old friction decision from successive production states. At 100 Hz the sum of positive friction contributions under midpoint kinetic-work accounting is **0.06538 J**, despite no external excitation; the initial spring energy is only **0.018 J**. Total energy nevertheless decays overall because other numerical/damping terms remove energy. A net decay or sign-count improvement alone would therefore be inadequate evidence of passive friction. Endpoint and midpoint work are both retained; neither is labeled exact continuous heat.

The former spring's coefficient changed with speed. If interpreted as storage U=k(v)δ²/2, its `δ² dk/2` contribution was not accounted. Its comments described assistance preventing off-center rest, not actual caster geometry. It is replaced by an explicitly declared fixed mechanical spring approximation with the same existing k/I setting and stationary stiffness. It remains optional through zero stiffness. This changes high-speed semantics deliberately and visibly; it is not claimed to reproduce the old assistance.

Driver control directly imposed δ and estimated ω=(δ_new−δ_old)/h. A fixed target jump thus produced rate proportional to Physics Hz. Release could inherit that arbitrarily large rate without an actuator impulse/work definition. Input also wrote the same HUD field that render interpolation temporarily overwrote and restored; events on frames without physics could lose the requested value. A separate sampled target and finite actuator solve remove both paths without estimating motion from event frequency.

Finally, implicit column viscosity does not implicitly couple the tire feedback. A one-tick delayed aligning moment can do the wrong work at coarse dt. The moving-release test isolates that remaining numerical defect at unchanged grip/trail. Tightening just column friction or damping it harder would not justify accepting its divergent 50 Hz result.

## Corrective architecture

The definitive equations, coordinate transformations, dependency diagram, integration proof and storage/work definitions are in [STEERING_MODEL.md](STEERING_MODEL.md). [PARAMETERS.md](PARAMETERS.md) documents units, classifications, ranges, implicit visual ratio, driver gains and compatibility.

The smallest defensible mechanical model retains one effective road-wheel degree of freedom. Exact affine propagation treats its physical spring, damping and finite driver impedance together between friction/stop events; static equilibrium and localized velocity zeros supply hold and sliding opposition. Mechanical stops use localized discrete impact intervals and unilateral persistent contact, with explicitly recorded kinetic loss. An additional undamped-spring regression rejected 6.06% prototype attenuation and led to analytic propagation rather than accepting a merely passive but inaccurate integrator. Passive isolated mechanical energy cannot increase, up to numerical roundoff; permitted spring oscillations remain in underdamped tests.

The demonstrated interface dependency is handled by extending the existing shared body/wheel contact root to a fourth unknown, front δ. The same accepted internal moment advances the column and the same candidate angle defines contact axes. No tire constitutive equation, gain or transient is changed. Integrating world impulses in their actual changing front frames is necessary: rotating an average longitudinal/lateral impulse afterward by the final angle would violate the shared body-impulse contract. The chassis still receives the integrated forces and uses Mission 01's unchanged rigid pose/history update.

All original tire/body adaptive thresholds are retained; additional steering angle/rate comparisons reject unresolved steering trajectories. There is no accepted failed root or hidden fallback to damping. Counters now include attempted evaluations, nonlinear iterations and rejected intervals as well as accepted subdivisions. Previously published accepted-evaluation counts did not include discarded adaptive trials; the report distinguishes them.

Sleep changes are confined to the application's existing history-reset contract in `engine.js`. Cached translation/yaw and derivative inputs now match the reset histories. Steering and tire memory are preserved; only the exported average SAT cache is invalidated when body transport is set to zero. The current solve reconstructs the moment on the next interval. The unchanged sleep thresholds remain a dissipative application intervention, with removed body energy diagnosed separately.

A small ledger residual alone only verifies discrete accounting.

Expected downstream changes are real off-center friction hold, less artificial stationary return, finite input response and tracking offset, no target-jump release velocity, changed high-speed spring contribution, removal of previous-tick steering feedback artifacts, and near-rest forces computed from consistent body caches. Reverse release and drift are not automatically steered to center. Stability of every uncalibrated trail/inertia/zero-damping combination is not claimed. Permitted physical oscillation and model-induced steering instability must be distinguished through refinement and eventual measured hardware data.

## Remaining limitations and defensible next steps

- **Open total-vehicle steering moment/work boundary.** The effective column receives pneumatic moment, but a complete rack/chassis mass matrix and finite-contact-patch yaw/work exchange do not exist. The ledger treats tire moment as measured external column work. The chassis still uses its inherited arm×force moment. Consequently there is no claim of conservation of total steering+chassis angular momentum or complete road heat. A consistent absolute wheel yaw `ψ+δ`, generalized torque mapping and calibrated finite-patch moment model are the defensible extension; simply adding an opposite yaw torque is not.
- **No static tire scrub, caster/jacking or steering linkage geometry.** Pneumatic transport vanishes at rest as specified in Mission 02. Column friction and the declared fixed spring are separate approximations. Ackermann differences become material at tight locks; introducing them requires the corresponding common-coordinate torque Jacobians. Neither is necessary to correct the reproduced friction and time-delay defects.
- **First-order outer geometry/load integration.** Internal steering/contact refinement cannot remove all outer pose/load splitting error. Very coarse timesteps, complex limit cycles and uncalibrated extremes require independent convergence checks. Mission 01's angular-sampling guard and coordinate cancellation limits remain.
- **Finite actuator authority and uncalibrated physical coefficients.** Fixed input-response gains are explicit, with no automatic SAT cancellation. Strong moments/static friction can offset target tracking or exceed finite authority. Neither these settings nor the existing column inertia/trail are validated against a real DeLorean or steering rack.
- **Sleep, drag, drivetrain and suspension remain simplified.** Sleep still removes small body kinetic energy. Wheel spin may wake a body through real contact; force memory is not globally wiped at a speed threshold. Exact tire static stiction, near-zero rolling-drag behavior, clutch/reflected-inertia defects and complete suspension work remain separate work.
- **Throughput is not ready for large AI batches.** Shared nonlinear steering/contact integration can be much more expensive than delayed steering, especially during rapid reversals and stiff transients. Measurements are explicit; there is no real-time or many-vehicle training guarantee. Analytic Jacobians, elimination of the extra column unknown where mathematically valid, event-aware integration and batched compiled kernels merit separate matched-accuracy work. Lowering friction accuracy, tire stiffness or damping to obtain throughput is not a correction.

Deliberately unchanged: peak/sliding tire grip, slip peaks, load sensitivity, magnitude relaxation, trail law and length defaults, wheel/contact brake equations, yaw-damping law/default, suspension equations/parameters, drivetrain/clutch/gear logic, chassis rigid integration/history/constraints/collisions, graphic controls/layout/rendering and audio. `engine.js` changes only sleep state synchronization and its diagnostic. Steering labels/descriptions and explicit import metadata change, while IDs, values and UI control counts remain compatible. No merge is performed.
