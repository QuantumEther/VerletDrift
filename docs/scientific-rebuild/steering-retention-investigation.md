# Steering retention and self-alignment investigation

The braking-and-tire-contact and tire-road-observatory mini-simulations do not currently contain the physical information needed to implement validated steering self-alignment. No steering behavior was changed in this investigation.

In both prototypes, `src/main.js` converts the held A/D keys into a signed steering command; releasing both produces zero. `src/vehicle.js` passes that command to `advanceSteering` in `src/steering.js`. That function treats the command as a column position target and rate-limits motion toward it, so neutral input actively returns the column to zero even at rest. The closed linkage computes distinct left and right angles from the column angle. The laboratory path in `src/planar.js` independently rate-limits its steering toward the commanded angle; it too returns to zero when the input is released. With the automobile linkage present, the planar value is overwritten by the average of the two linkage angles. The road-wheel drawings use the resulting per-wheel steering angles; the automobile view uses the linkage state.

The steering linkage is explicitly massless and has no angular velocity, inertia, driver torque, damping, friction, or mechanical back-drive model. The planar contact solver resolves longitudinal and lateral tire forces and wheel spin, but has no steering degree of freedom in its generalized velocity or contact Jacobians, no steering-axis reaction moment, and no tire aligning moment or pneumatic-trail state. The suspension/contact geometry does not define caster, steering-axis inclination, contact-patch offset, mechanical trail, or their variation with load and steering. The drawn kingpins are graphical objects, not mechanical geometry. The reduced tire law and the documented absence of tire relaxation and aligning moment do not determine how a pneumatic moment behaves across forward, zero, and reverse rolling.

To proceed without inventing physical accuracy, the model needs a specified steering-axis/contact geometry and mechanical trail, a justified tire aligning-moment model with pneumatic trail and reverse/near-zero-speed behavior, and steering/linkage inertia, damping, static and dynamic friction, driver torque and back-drive characteristics. The linkage's angle Jacobians must map each wheel's contact moment to the column, and the coupled solver must account for steering velocity, contact work and reactions consistently. Parameter sources and operating limits must be documented. The required stationary, rolling, reversing, grip, symmetry, friction, energy and timestep-refinement tests can then exercise that coupled model. Simply retaining the last keyboard target or multiplying lateral force by an assumed trail would not establish the requested physical behavior.

The larger browser simulator has a separate steering implementation and pneumatic-trail approximation, but those parameters and its low-speed fallback spring do not supply validated steering geometry or a reverse-capable contact-moment model for these mini-simulations.
