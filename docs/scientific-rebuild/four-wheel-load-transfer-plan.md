# Proposed next experiment: four-wheel load transfer

**Planning only.** This experiment would be laboratory No. 10; no new vehicle implementation or physics is introduced by this document. Its purpose is controlled measurement of four corner loads and grip, not a claim of vehicle-specific fidelity.

## Existing model audit

- `braking-and-tire-contact-prototype/src/vehicle.js` integrates chassis heave/pitch and two axle unsprung states using springs, dampers, compliant vertical contact and a 1 ms RK4 step. It divides each axle normal force equally between its two wheels. Its comment explicitly excludes longitudinal pitch coupling; there is no chassis roll, independent four-corner suspension, lateral load transfer or lateral terrain variation.
- `planar.js` and `contact-solver.js` solve four wheel spin, longitudinal/lateral motion, yaw, clutch, steering and friction-limited braking impulses together. The tire law is a viscous contact capped by μN; fixed μ means peak force scales linearly with load, not load-sensitive friction. Four wheel forces, load, slip, and utilization are observable, but normal loads originate in the separate vertical step. The reduced horizontal energy ledger does not close vertical spring, tire and gravity work or pitch/roll reactions.
- The existing closed steering linkage and contact trail map column motion into each front contact; its inertia, friction, torque and steering-stop accounting are reusable. The engine, drivetrain and hydraulic braking can provide applied torques through their existing ports. Do not add a second drivetrain, steering model, direct yaw assist or extra load-transfer force on top of chassis reactions.
- `TireObservatory`, accepted-stage capture and bounded 2D/3D track rendering can be reused. Their force-stage and geometry-stage samples must reflect the new four-contact points and only accepted integration stages.

## Coherent extension

Create an isolated experiment derived from the current braking/observatory exhibit. Give the sprung chassis heave z, pitch θ and roll φ, with independent wheel vertical positions and velocities at coordinates (x_i,y_i). For a declared small-angle model, mount height is z + θ x_i + φ y_i, with matching mount velocity. Use one spring/damper per corner from relative suspension deflection and velocity, and unilateral tire normal contact based on local road height and relative normal velocity. Apply each suspension force and its moment to the chassis and equal-and-opposite force to the corresponding unsprung mass. Define signs once from virtual work; do not introduce both an analytical transfer formula and a physical reaction for the same force.

Compute longitudinal and lateral tire-contact reaction moments from the actual force application point relative to the sprung and unsprung bodies, including wheel/brake and drivetrain reactions needed for a closed momentum and work ledger. Decide explicitly how wheel hubs, steering axes, gravity and tire contact points move under pitch/roll; retain the existing steering coordinate and trail mapping. Extend the coupled contact solve and/or use a convergent, energy-audited partitioned method; specify time levels and interface work before selecting the integrator. Avoid using instantaneous acceleration-derived load shifts as additional forces. Normal contact is unilateral and may unload; validate no tensile reaction.

Introduce one parameterized, continuous load-sensitive friction curve μ(N) with μ(N_ref)=μ_ref, finite nonnegative force and zero force for N≤0. Specify bounded load range, combined-slip behavior and parameter provenance before implementation. Test monotonic reduction of μ with increasing load over its calibrated range; do not infer actual tire performance from illustrative coefficients. Reuse the existing friction envelope/solver only after its variable-load and convexity properties are established. An alternative sourced tire model requires a separate validated fixture before vehicle integration.

## Parameters and limits

Initial geometry, masses, stiffnesses and damping may use the current scaled exhibit as **illustrative reference values**, extended to individual corners with documented static equilibrium and units. CG height, track widths, pitch/roll inertia, corner spring and damping rates, tire vertical properties and load sensitivity need explicit values and provenance. Where measurements are unavailable, publish ranges and sensitivity sweeps; do not label them as a particular car. Clearly state whether anti-roll bars, suspension kinematics, tire relaxation, aero, roll steer, camber and nonlinear bump stops are omitted. If an anti-roll bar is later included, its paired equal/opposite spring reactions and stored energy must appear exactly once.

## Acceptance gates

1. Static equilibrium: four nonnegative loads sum to total weight; symmetry and expected front/rear distribution hold; spring/tire compression balances forces. Zero-load contacts produce no tangential wrench.
2. Isolated pitch/roll and suspension oscillations: conservative energy converges under refinement; dampers dissipate nonnegative work; rigid translation/rotation and symmetric corner swaps obey momentum and symmetry.
3. Controlled braking and acceleration: forward/reverse and left/right cases transfer load with the expected sign; total normal reaction and chassis/unsprung moment balance close within measured tolerances. Distinguish transient suspension response from a steady quasi-static reference ΔF≈m a h/L; this formula is a test reference, not an added force.
4. Constant-radius and transient steering: outer/inner loads respond with the expected sign and mirror across turn direction; compare steady total lateral transfer against m a_y h/track under declared simplifying assumptions. Verify lift-off, independent utilization and combined-slip bounds.
5. Load sensitivity: equal total load split unevenly yields the analytically expected change in total available grip for the selected curve; also test fixed-μ mode, low/zero grip and supported/unloaded transitions.
6. Closed energy ledger includes gravity, sprung/unsprung kinetic and potential energy, tire/suspension elastic energy, actuator work, tire/brake/damper dissipation and separately calculated numerical loss. Quantitative tolerances must be established from reference fixtures before tuning; no balancing residual or disabled assertions.
7. Replay identical inputs at h, h/2 and h/4 over equal physical time, including brake release, reverse, steering reversal, road bump and contact loss. Report load peaks, time histories, energy residuals and convergence rates, not only pass/fail. Compare accepted observer work and track positions against independently calculated contact geometry; ordinary rolling must not deposit rubber.
8. Preserve existing braking, steering, drivetrain, rendering, history-budget and observer regression suites; compare the new model in an explicitly configured reduced symmetric case rather than forcing equality when coupling differs.
